Snowball: The 2022 Huizhou Quarantine Case

The killing of a three-year-old Samoyed by anti-epidemic workers while his owners were in quarantine.

Case Summary

Snowball was a three-year-old Samoyed living with his owners in Huizhou, Guangdong. On 2 March 2022, the owner’s boyfriend tested positive for COVID-19 and was sent to a designated hospital. The owner was also required to quarantine as a close contact, leaving Snowball alone in the apartment.

Later that day, two anti-epidemic workers entered the home to disinfect it. Before they arrived, Snowball’s owner told them which room he was in and explained that he was harmless and would not bite. According to the messages later shared publicly, she believed that Snowball would be tested for COVID-19 rather than killed.

The owners watched through their home security system as the workers struck Snowball repeatedly with a long object and killed him. The footage and screenshots of the owner’s messages were posted on Weibo on 4 March and spread widely, prompting public outrage.

Background

The incident occurred during China’s strict COVID-19 containment period, when people who tested positive and their close contacts could be removed from their homes for centralized treatment or quarantine.

Snowball’s owner had informed the workers in advance that a dog was inside the apartment. Screenshots reported by English-language media showed that she identified the room where he was staying and said that he was not aggressive. The workers nevertheless killed him without first testing him for COVID-19.

Snowball’s death followed the November 2021 killing of a pet corgi in Shangrao, Jiangxi, under similar quarantine circumstances. The recurrence of such incidents intensified concern over the treatment of companion animals during pandemic-control operations.

Official Response

On 5 March 2022, the official Weibo account of the Huizhou Propaganda Department released a statement confirming that the incident had occurred and apologizing to Snowball’s owners.

The statement acknowledged that the workers had used a “cruel manner” to kill Snowball. It also claimed that the dog had been exposed to COVID-19 for an extended period through the owner’s boyfriend and therefore had a high likelihood of infection.

However, reporting stated that Snowball was not tested before he was killed. Authorities said that the two workers involved had been suspended from their duties and that officials had contacted the owners to apologize.

Public Response

Online outrage

The footage and the owner’s account generated widespread anger on Weibo. Many users were distressed not only by the killing itself but also by the fact that the owner had clearly warned the workers that Snowball was inside and was not aggressive.

Some users said that their own dogs reacted fearfully to the sound of Snowball’s cries in the surveillance footage. Others questioned why another pet had been killed during quarantine enforcement only months after the Shangrao corgi case.

Calls for legal protection

The case renewed calls for laws prohibiting animal cruelty and for clear nationwide procedures governing the care of companion animals when owners are removed for quarantine.

Reporting also described the removal of related hashtag pages from Weibo and the closure of comments beneath the official statement. These actions further intensified criticism from members of the public.

Verification Notes

This page is based on publicly available reporting, screenshots and footage shared by Snowball’s owner, and the official response quoted by news outlets. Voices For Animals CN has not independently inspected the apartment, interviewed the workers, or reviewed internal government records.

Both cited English-language reports identify Snowball as a three-year-old Samoyed, state that he was killed on 2 March 2022 while his owners were in quarantine, and report that two workers were later suspended.

The official statement reportedly claimed that Snowball was likely to have contracted COVID-19 because of his exposure to an infected household member. The cited reporting states that he was not tested before he was killed.

The sources reviewed for this page do not report a criminal prosecution or a final legal judgment against the workers. This archive therefore does not describe the suspensions as criminal accountability.
