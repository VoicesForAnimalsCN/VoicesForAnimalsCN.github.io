Chaofen: The 2021 Shangrao Corgi Case

The killing of a pet corgi by pandemic-control workers while the owner was held in centralized quarantine.

Authorities Apologized · Personnel Reassigned

Case Summary

On 12 November 2021, residents of the Jinfeng Garden residential compound in Shangrao, Jiangxi, were transferred to hotels for centralized quarantine after COVID-19 cases were detected in the area.

One resident, identified in reporting as Ms. Fu, left her pet corgi Chaofen at home after being told by a pandemic-control worker that the dog would not be harmed. The quarantine hotel reportedly did not permit pets.

Later that afternoon, Ms. Fu watched through a home security camera as workers wearing protective clothing entered her apartment. Footage showed a worker striking Chaofen on the head with a metal rod. The dog ran out of the camera’s view, and the owner later reported that Chaofen had been killed.

The footage spread widely online and prompted public outrage. Local authorities later stated that workers had carried out a “harmless disposal” of the dog without adequately communicating with the owner.

Background

The case occurred during a period of strict COVID-19 containment measures in China. Entire residential buildings or compounds could be placed under control, and residents considered close contacts could be transferred to centralized quarantine facilities.

According to reporting, Ms. Fu asked a worker on the morning of 12 November what would happen to Chaofen. She was reportedly told that the dog would not be harmed. She also tested negative for COVID-19 within the relevant period.

Several hours after residents left, workers entered apartments to conduct disinfection. In Ms. Fu’s home, the workers opened the locked door in the presence of police and encountered Chaofen inside.

The killing became an early and widely reported example of the risks faced by companion animals when owners were removed from their homes under pandemic-control rules. A similar case involving the Samoyed Snowball occurred in Huizhou in March 2022.

Present

Chaofen’s death continues to be cited in discussions about companion-animal protection, emergency planning, government accountability, and China’s lack of a nationwide anti-cruelty law.

Official Response

The epidemic-control headquarters of Xishi Subdistrict in Xinzhou District issued a statement after the footage drew widespread attention.

The statement said that residents had been instructed not to lock their doors so that workers could enter to disinfect their homes. When workers found Ms. Fu’s door locked, they reportedly contacted police and opened it in an officer’s presence.

Officials stated that the workers then carried out a “harmless disposal” of the dog without fully communicating with the owner. The statement did not describe a veterinary examination, COVID-19 test, or individualized assessment of Chaofen before the killing.

Authorities said that the personnel involved had been criticized and reassigned and that they were ordered to apologize. The official statement also asserted that the owner had accepted the apology and expressed understanding of the pandemic-control measures.

Public Response

Widespread outrage

The surveillance footage provoked anger across Chinese social media. Many users questioned why a healthy household pet had been killed when the owner had tested negative and had been told that the dog would not be harmed.

The official use of the term “harmless disposal” was also heavily criticized. Commenters argued that bureaucratic language obscured the violent manner in which the dog was killed and failed to acknowledge the animal as a family member.

Concern among pet owners

The case heightened fear among pet owners who worried about what would happen to their animals if they were sent into centralized quarantine. Some began seeking foster homes, emergency contacts, or written care arrangements.

Calls for national procedures

Animal-welfare advocates and legal experts called for clear nationwide rules requiring humane care, testing, temporary boarding, and owner notification before any action involving pets during a public-health emergency.

Verification Notes

This page is based on publicly available reporting, the owner’s account and home-surveillance footage as described by news organizations, and the official local response reproduced by Chinese media.

The cited sources consistently report that the owner was moved to centralized quarantine, that workers entered the apartment, and that surveillance footage showed a worker striking the corgi with a metal rod.

The dog’s name is rendered as Chaofen, a romanization of 炒粉. Some English-language reports refer to the animal only as “the corgi” and do not use the name.

Local authorities used the term “harmless disposal.” This archive uses direct language when describing the observable conduct shown in the footage and reported by multiple sources.

The sources reviewed for this page do not report a criminal prosecution or final legal judgment against the workers. Reassignment and an ordered apology are therefore not described here as criminal accountability.
