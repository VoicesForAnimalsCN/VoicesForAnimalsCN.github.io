Case Summary

Wangwang was a community dog living in Pingpu Village in Jieyang, Guangdong. Local residents reportedly knew and regularly fed her. At the time of the incident, she was caring for three young puppies.

On 28 June 2026, Wangwang and her puppies were killed in an act of cruelty involving a group of minors. Video recordings of the incident circulated online and led to widespread public anger in mainland China and abroad.

The case renewed public discussion about juvenile accountability, online circulation of animal-abuse content, and the absence of comprehensive nationwide anti-cruelty legislation in China.

Background

According to published reports, Wangwang was a stray mother dog living in a rural community in Xinheng Town. Villagers regularly provided her with food, and she had recently given birth to three puppies.

Some reports also stated that she had previously been mistreated by members of the same group. Because the available information comes from media reports, public statements, and accounts circulated online, this archive distinguishes confirmed information from claims that have not been independently verified.

Official Response

Local public-security authorities reportedly opened an investigation after the recordings circulated online. On 30 June 2026, the Xinheng Town government published a notice stating that a task force had been established.

The notice said that schools were directed to strengthen education, that the parents were urged to fulfil their guardianship responsibilities, and that corrective educational measures had been arranged.

Published reports have not been fully consistent about the number of minors involved or the exact implementation of the announced measures. These discrepancies are noted in the verification section below.

Public and International Response

Mainland China

The case generated widespread anger on social media. Many participants called for stronger protections against animal cruelty and questioned whether existing educational and guardianship measures were sufficient.

Supporters shared awareness materials and funded public advertisements. Reports also described restrictions on online and offline organizing related to the case.

Hong Kong and Taiwan

Animal-welfare organizations, public figures, and media outlets in Hong Kong and Taiwan responded publicly to the incident. Debate also emerged over whether graphic footage should remain online as evidence or be removed to reduce distress and discourage imitation.

International response

International animal-welfare groups issued statements and appeals, while several online petitions called for accountability and nationwide animal-protection legislation in China.

Verification Notes

This page is based on publicly available reporting, official notices, organizational statements, and archived online materials. Voices For Animals CN has not independently investigated the scene or interviewed all parties involved.

Sources differ on certain details, including whether four or five minors were involved and whether all announced corrective measures were ultimately carried out. For that reason, this page uses the wording “a group of minors” unless a statement is attributed to a specific source.

This archive does not publish the identities, addresses, school information, or other identifying information of minors.
