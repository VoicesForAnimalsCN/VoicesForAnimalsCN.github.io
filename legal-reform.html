Current Status

No unified national anti-cruelty law

China has not enacted a comprehensive national law that generally prohibits cruelty toward all animals or creates a unified welfare system for companion animals.

This page documents proposals, official responses, and public discussion. It does not describe enacted legislation unless a source is specifically identified as an existing law.

In a 2019 response to a proposal submitted during the Second Session of the 13th National People's Congress, the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs stated that China had not enacted a unified anti-cruelty law.

The Ministry emphasized that animal-related protections were already distributed across laws and regulations concerning livestock, wildlife, laboratory animals, zoo animals, disease prevention, and other regulated settings.

That response illustrates the central reform question: whether the existing subject-specific framework should remain divided or be supplemented by broader national standards addressing cruelty, welfare, enforcement, and companion animals.

Why Reform Is Proposed

Existing Chinese laws regulate animals according to specific purposes such as disease control, wildlife conservation, livestock production, food safety, public health, and laboratory management.

Critics of the current framework argue that this approach leaves significant gaps where conduct causes suffering but does not clearly fall within wildlife, epidemic-control, livestock, property, or public-order rules.

Companion animals are frequently central to this discussion. Dogs and cats may be protected indirectly through ownership, theft, property-damage, quarantine, local dog-management, or public-order provisions, but there is no single national statute establishing general welfare standards for them.

Reform proposals therefore commonly focus on legal definitions, minimum care standards, deliberate cruelty, transport, commercial breeding, abandonment, emergency response, enforcement authority, and penalties.

Reform Timeline

Before 2019

Academic and public proposals developed

Legal scholars, animal-welfare advocates, and members of the public circulated proposals for broader animal protection and anti-cruelty legislation.

Many documents from this period were expert proposals, discussion drafts, or advocacy materials rather than bills formally adopted by the national legislature.

2019

A National People's Congress proposal received an official response

A representative submitted Proposal No. 5074 concerning animal rights and the creation of a law prohibiting animal cruelty during the Second Session of the 13th National People's Congress.

The Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs replied that China had not enacted a unified anti-cruelty law, while describing protections found in existing sector-specific laws, regulations, and administrative measures.

February 2020

Wildlife-trade reform accelerated

The Standing Committee of the National People's Congress adopted a decision strengthening restrictions on illegal wildlife trade and the consumption of terrestrial wildlife in response to public-health and ecological concerns.

This was a major legal development concerning wildlife, but it did not create a general anti-cruelty law for companion animals.

2021–2022

Companion-animal cases renewed public debate

Cases including the Shangrao corgi and Snowball prompted renewed public discussion about legal authority, proportionality, procedural safeguards, and the treatment of companion animals during emergency or administrative interventions.

Public reaction to individual cases is evidence of social concern, but it should not be confused with formal legislative action.

2026

Calls for stronger protection continued

The deaths of Wangwang and her puppies, together with concern surrounding large-scale cat transport cases, again led members of the public to call for clearer protection, stronger accountability, and enforceable anti-cruelty standards.

Present

No unified national statute has been identified

The archive has not identified an enacted comprehensive national animal-protection or general anti-cruelty law covering companion animals.

Future official proposals, draft laws, legislative-plan entries, ministry responses, and enacted amendments will be added when reliable documentation becomes available.

Common Reform Themes

Cruelty

Prohibition of Deliberate Harm

Establishing a clear legal prohibition against intentional cruelty, severe neglect, and the infliction of unnecessary suffering.

Reform theme · Not a statement of current national law
Companion Animals

Basic Protection for Dogs and Cats

Creating minimum standards for ownership, care, abandonment, commercial breeding, sale, transport, rescue, and emergency treatment.

Reform theme · Scope varies across proposals
Enforcement

Clear Authority and Penalties

Identifying responsible agencies, establishing reporting and investigation procedures, preserving evidence, and providing proportionate administrative or criminal penalties.

Reform theme · Institutional design remains debated
Welfare Standards

Housing, Transport, and Handling

Setting minimum requirements for space, ventilation, food, water, sanitation, veterinary care, handling, transport duration, and emergency response.

Reform theme · Standards may differ by animal category
Commerce

Breeding and Trade Regulation

Regulating commercial breeders, dealers, online sales, animal markets, traceability, quarantine documentation, and the sale of animals from unclear or unlawful sources.

Reform theme · May overlap with existing business rules
Public Administration

Procedural Safeguards

Requiring lawful authority, written records, proportional measures, owner notification, veterinary assessment, and review procedures when animals are seized, removed, or killed.

Reform theme · Particularly relevant to emergency cases

Public Discussion

Public debate frequently intensifies after widely shared cases involving the death, abuse, removal, theft, or transport of dogs and cats.

Common questions include whether deliberate cruelty should be independently punishable, which agency should investigate, how companion animals should be treated during emergencies, how stolen animals should be identified, and whether shelters and rescue organizations should receive formal recognition.

Online petitions, open letters, social-media campaigns, and individual proposals can demonstrate public concern. They do not, by themselves, establish that a proposal has entered the national legislative process.

This archive distinguishes among public advocacy, academic proposals, recommendations submitted to government, official responses, formal draft legislation, and enacted law.

Limits and Unresolved Questions

Definition and scope

A future law would need to define which animals are covered and how standards differ among companion animals, livestock, wildlife, laboratory animals, working animals, and animals used in entertainment or education.

Relationship with existing laws

Any broader law would need to operate alongside the Animal Epidemic Prevention Law, Wildlife Protection Law, Animal Husbandry Law, food-safety rules, local dog-management regulations, property law, and criminal law.

Enforcement responsibility

Reform would require decisions about which agencies receive complaints, investigate evidence, remove animals from danger, issue penalties, supervise shelters, and coordinate with police, veterinary authorities, and local governments.

Evidence and proportionality

Legal standards would also need to address veterinary evidence, digital evidence, owner identification, emergency necessity, seizure powers, due process, and proportionate penalties.

Verification Notes

Inclusion on this page does not mean that a proposal has been introduced as a bill, placed on a legislative agenda, approved, or enacted.

The 2019 entry is based on an official Ministry response to an NPC representative's proposal. The response confirms the absence of a unified anti-cruelty law at that time and describes the government's sector-specific approach.

The 2020 wildlife decision and later Wildlife Protection Law are significant legal developments, but their subject is wildlife and public-health regulation. They should not be described as a general companion-animal protection law.

Case-related entries document public debate generated by archived incidents. They do not establish that the cases directly produced a formal legislative proposal.

English titles and summaries are descriptive translations prepared by this archive. The linked Chinese-language documents remain the primary sources.
