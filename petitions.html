Overview

This page indexes public campaigns connected to animal protection in China. Petitions may be created by individuals, informal groups, advocacy organizations, or campaign platforms.

A petition documents public concern but does not itself verify the factual claims it contains, prove legal responsibility, or establish that the named recipient has formally accepted or reviewed the request.

Readers should review the petition text, cited evidence, organizer identity, recipient, privacy terms, and requested action before signing or sharing.

How to Evaluate a Petition

Question What to Check Why It Matters
Who created it? Organizer name, organization, contact details, and history of updates. Helps establish responsibility and credibility.
What evidence is cited? Original reporting, official records, dates, photographs, and clearly labeled allegations. Reduces the risk of spreading unsupported claims.
Who is the recipient? Whether the named institution has authority to take the requested action. A campaign is more meaningful when directed to an appropriate decision-maker.
What is being requested? Specific, lawful, proportionate, and realistically enforceable action. Vague demands can be difficult to evaluate or implement.
How is personal data used? Platform privacy policy, public display of names, email use, and campaign communications. Petition platforms may collect and process personal data.

Verification Notes

Petition wording, signature counts, organizers, recipients, and campaign status may change after this page is published.

Inclusion does not imply that every allegation in a petition has been independently verified or that the archive endorses all requested actions.

A platform page is not an official government record. Formal legislative status should be checked through government and legislative sources.

Campaigns containing harassment, threats, doxxing, discriminatory language, or unsupported personal accusations should not be promoted by this archive.
