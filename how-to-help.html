Overview

Effective advocacy begins with accurate information, proportional action, and respect for the safety and privacy of everyone directly affected.

Sharing an unverified allegation, exposing a private address, encouraging confrontation, or identifying the wrong person can cause serious harm and weaken legitimate efforts to protect animals.

The steps below focus on documentation, preservation, responsible sharing, appropriate reporting, and support for established organizations.

Do not place yourself or an animal in additional danger

Do not trespass, pursue vehicles, confront suspected offenders, interfere with an active police response, or attempt a rescue beyond your training and legal authority.

Document Responsibly

Record the original source

Save the full URL, account or publisher name, visible publication date, access date, language, and the specific claim the material supports.

Keep original files when lawfully available

Avoid repeatedly editing, cropping, compressing, or adding text over the only copy. Keep a separate unmodified original and create edited copies for publication.

Preserve context

Record surrounding captions, preceding and following posts, location claims, time references, and whether the material is first-hand, reposted, translated, or anonymous.

Separate observation from conclusion

Describe what is visibly documented before stating what may have happened. Label allegations, estimates, translations, and unconfirmed identities clearly.

Preserve Sources

The Internet Archive's Wayback Machine offers a “Save Page Now” function for capturing an individual webpage. It may not preserve every image, video, comment, login-only page, or interactive element.

WITNESS publishes an Activists' Guide to Archiving Video, including guidance on planning, metadata, storage, file organization, and long-term preservation.

Share Carefully

Use Original Links

Direct Readers to Sources

Link to the original article, official statement, or public post whenever possible instead of circulating detached screenshots.

Archived links may be added when the original becomes unavailable.
Protect Privacy

Remove Unnecessary Personal Information

Do not publish private addresses, phone numbers, identification documents, unrelated family information, or medical details.

Public-interest documentation does not require indiscriminate exposure.
Use Accurate Language

Label Uncertainty

Use terms such as “reported,” “alleged,” “according to,” “preliminary,” and “not independently confirmed” when the evidence requires them.

Do not present a rumor as an official finding.
Avoid Graphic Repetition

Use Content Warnings

Share only as much graphic material as is necessary for documentation. Consider still images, descriptions, blurring, and clear warnings.

Repeated graphic circulation can harm viewers and affected owners.

Report Through Appropriate Channels

The correct reporting channel depends on location, immediate danger, ownership, suspected theft, disease risk, wildlife involvement, transport, food safety, and the identity of the responsible authority.

Where safe and lawful, preserve evidence before reporting. Provide concise facts: what happened, where, when, which animals are involved, whether danger is ongoing, and how the evidence was obtained.

An international organization or online campaign may not be able to dispatch local rescuers or compel government action. Emergency situations generally require local authorities, veterinary services, property owners, or established local rescue networks.

Support Responsible Organizations

Support may include sharing verified educational material, translating accurately, volunteering within your skills, fostering through an established group, donating after checking financial transparency, or helping preserve records.

Before sending money, confirm that the payment recipient is authorized, the fundraiser identifies a responsible organization or individual, updates are provided, and the requested expenses are explained.

What to Avoid

Avoid Why Safer Alternative
Doxxing or publishing private details Can endanger uninvolved people and create legal risk. Preserve privately and provide relevant details only to appropriate authorities or verified investigators.
Harassment and threats Can escalate danger, undermine evidence, and harm legitimate advocacy. Use factual public communication and lawful reporting channels.
Identifying minors or alleged offenders without verification Creates privacy, safety, and misidentification risks. Use non-identifying descriptions unless a reliable public record lawfully establishes identity.
Reposting without source or date Removes context and makes verification difficult. Share the original link, date, account, and a clear source description.
Donating to an unverified personal account Funds may be misdirected or impossible to track. Confirm authorization, identity, budget, updates, and organization records.

Verification Notes

This page provides general documentation and advocacy guidance. It is not legal advice, emergency-response instruction, or a guarantee that any reporting channel will act.

Internet Archive captures and screenshots may be useful for historical reference but can omit content and may require additional authentication for legal use.

WITNESS materials were developed for preserving human-rights video, but many principles concerning original files, metadata, context, and storage are also useful for responsible public-interest documentation.

Always consider local law, platform rules, personal safety, animal safety, privacy, and the wishes of directly affected owners.
